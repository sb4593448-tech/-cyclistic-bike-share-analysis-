# Cyclistic Bike-Share Case Study: Converting Casual Riders into Annual Members

*A Google Data Analytics Capstone Project*


---

## 1. Introduction

Cyclistic is a bike-share program operating a fleet of 5,824 bicycles across 692 docking stations in Chicago. Since 2016, Cyclistic has grown into a flexible, inclusive mobility service offering three pricing options: single-ride passes, full-day passes, and annual memberships. Riders who purchase single-ride or full-day passes are referred to as **casual riders**; riders who purchase annual memberships are **Cyclistic members**.

Cyclistic's finance team has concluded that annual members are substantially more profitable than casual riders. Rather than pursuing new customer acquisition broadly, the Director of Marketing, **Lily Moreno**, believes the most effective growth lever is **converting existing casual riders into annual members**.

---

## 2. Ask Phase

### Business Task
Analyze historical Cyclistic trip data to understand how **annual members** and **casual riders** use the service differently, and translate those behavioral differences into a data-driven marketing strategy to convert casual riders into annual members.

### Key Business Questions
1. How do annual members and casual riders use Cyclistic bikes differently?
2. Why would casual riders buy a Cyclistic annual membership?
3. How can Cyclistic use digital media to influence casual riders to become members?

*(This case study focuses on Question 1, which directly informs the marketing recommendations in Section 6.)*

## 3. Prepare Phase

**Data source:** Cyclistic's historical trip data, made publicly available by Motivate International Inc. under license, hosted at the [Divvy trip data S3 bucket](https://divvy-tripdata.s3.amazonaws.com/index.html).

**Scope:** The most recent 12 months of monthly trip data (`YYYYMM-divvy-tripdata.zip`), covering roughly 4–6 million individual rides.

**ROCCC assessment:**
- **Reliable:** First-party operational data generated by the bike-share system itself.
- **Original:** Sourced directly from the data provider, not a third-party aggregator.
- **Comprehensive:** Includes ride ID, bike type, start/end timestamps, start/end stations, coordinates, and user type — sufficient to answer the business questions.
- **Current:** Rolling 12-month window, refreshed monthly.
- **Cited:** Publicly licensed by Motivate International Inc.

**Limitations:** No personally identifiable information is included, so it is not possible to determine whether a single casual rider took multiple trips, or to cross-reference riders with home addresses/demographics. This limits the analysis to *behavioral* patterns rather than individual-level segmentation.

**Storage/access approach:** To avoid manually downloading and storing ~1–2 GB of raw CSVs locally, all 12 monthly ZIP files are streamed directly from S3 into memory inside Google Colab using `requests` + `BytesIO` + `zipfile`, then loaded straight into pandas. See `cyclistic_colab_script.py`.

## 4. Process Phase

Cleaning and transformation steps applied (fully scripted in `cyclistic_colab_script.py`):

1. **Parsed** `started_at` and `ended_at` as datetime objects.
2. **Calculated** `ride_length_min` = (`ended_at` − `started_at`) in minutes.
3. **Engineered** `day_of_week`, `month`, and `hour` columns from `started_at` for time-based analysis.
4. **Removed** rides with zero or negative duration (clock errors) and rides longer than 24 hours (likely docking/maintenance anomalies rather than genuine trips).
5. **Filtered out** test and maintenance rides (station names containing "test", "repair," or staff-only identifiers).
6. **Deduplicated** on `ride_id`.

## 7. Act Phase — Recommendations

Based strictly on the findings above, three to four recommendations to convert casual riders into annual members:

1. **Launch a "Weekend Warrior" membership tier or promotion.** Since casual usage peaks on weekends and skews toward longer, leisure-oriented rides, offer a discounted membership trial (e.g., first month free or reduced rate) specifically targeted at weekend casual riders, with messaging that emphasizes unlimited weekend rides at member pricing.

2. **Run a seasonal conversion campaign at peak casual-usage months.** Since casual ridership spikes sharply in `[X]`–`[X]`, concentrate acquisition spend and in-app/dock-side messaging in the weeks just before and during that window, when intent to ride is already high — the moment casual riders are most receptive to a membership pitch.

3. **Target high-traffic casual-rider stations with geo-fenced digital ads.** Use the `[X]` top casual-ride start stations (from the SQL query in `cyclistic_sql_queries.sql`, Section 7) to place station-adjacent signage, QR-code sign-up prompts, or geo-targeted social ads that highlight the cost savings of membership for riders who already frequent that station.

4. **Reframe membership value around commute savings, not just leisure.** Because members concentrate around clear commute-hour peaks (`[X]` and `[X]`) while casual riders don't, target casual riders who show repeat weekday riding patterns with a calculator-style ad ("You've spent $`[X]` on single rides this month — membership would have cost $`[X]`"), directly quantifying the savings a commuting-style casual rider is leaving on the table.
